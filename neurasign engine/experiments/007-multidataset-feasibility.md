# Multi-dataset feasibility review

Status: source/documentation review on 2026-09-26. No new recordings have been downloaded, merged or trained in this review. Participant counts below are published cohort sizes, not verified usable training counts.

The completed UNIVERSE/SWELL/Jev experiments do not establish a reliable six-output interpreter. They also do not establish that additional people, better physiological representations or compatible external supervision cannot improve it. A controlled multi-dataset experiment is a reasonable next step.

The existing SWELL spreadsheet remains a separate sensor-domain benchmark: chest ECG and finger EDA are not interchangeable with wrist PPG/EDA. Its native units and research-use restrictions remain unresolved for product deployment. Do not pool its three columns with canonical wrist measurements merely because their names look similar.

## Concrete next experiment

1. **Audit each source before extracting labels.** Record archive/file hashes, participant/session identity, device and body location, signal units/rates, timestamps, missing channels, label instrument/subscale, rating direction, timing and access terms. Detect repackaged duplicate recordings across repositories.
2. **Freeze new test people and external datasets before inspecting outcomes.** People and all their overlapping windows remain in one partition. A held-out dataset is also excluded from self-supervised pretraining and normalization in an inductive evaluation. Existing UNIVERSE/SWELL benchmarks have already been inspected repeatedly.
3. **Implement explicit signal profiles.** At minimum wrist PPG; wrist PPG+EDA; and wrist PPG+EDA+motion, with temperature only where actually recorded. Reuse one causal extraction definition per profile. Missing channels remain missing, with an explicit mask or a separately trained compatible profile. Never fabricate a complete v3 vector.
4. **Preserve instrument semantics.** Rescale a 0–10 subscale to 0–100 only after confirming the same instrument, anchors and direction. Do not equate RSME, UNIVERSE's five effort levels, task difficulty, NASA-TLX total, fatigue or stress. Train each output only on compatible observed labels; use separate heads for incompatible instruments. Do not add excluded affect/emotion targets.
5. **Compare incremental hypotheses.** First compare current-style models trained on one compatible dataset versus pooled datasets, with equal influence across studies/people and train-only preprocessing. Then test a shared physiological encoder pretrained on eligible raw recordings without labels, followed by output-specific labeled training. Extra unlabeled hours can support representation learning; they do not create missing ground-truth ratings.
6. **Measure whether pooling helps.** Evaluate both new people within studies and entirely held-out studies/devices, per target and input profile. Keep simple constants and single-dataset models as comparators. Report MAE, defined tolerance agreement, coverage and participant-level variation. Add a learning curve by number of training people and studies. Study/device identities are audit/grouping metadata, not shortcuts for predicting ratings.

This plan seeks more independent people, devices and tasks while keeping labels meaningful. A larger pool can help or hurt; the experiment must establish the direction. There is no justified target accuracy percentage before this evaluation.

## Pretraining reference

[Large-scale Training of Foundation Models for Wearable Biosignals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05409) demonstrates self-supervised learning from unlabeled wearable PPG/ECG. It motivates trying physiological pretraining, but does not establish that its data or trained weights are available to this project, nor that it solves our questionnaire targets.
